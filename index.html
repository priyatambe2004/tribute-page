1931 to 2015

Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam

Rocket scientist, teacher, and the 11th President of India, remembered as the "Missile Man of India".

Biography

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, into a modest family. As a boy he sold newspapers to help with household expenses, and he stayed curious about flight by watching birds along the coast.

He studied physics at St. Joseph's College in Tiruchirappalli and then aeronautical engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology. He began his career in 1958 at the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) and moved to the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) in 1969.

At ISRO he led the project that produced India's first indigenous satellite launch vehicle, the SLV-III. Later he guided India's missile programme and served as Principal Scientific Adviser to the Government of India. In 2002 he was elected President, and he served until 2007. Many people knew him as the "People's President" for his habit of meeting students and young people.

After his term he returned to teaching and writing. He died on 27 July 2015 in Shillong, while giving a lecture to students at the Indian Institute of Management.

Achievements

  • Led India's first indigenous satellite launch vehicle As project director of SLV-III, he helped place the Rohini satellite in orbit in July 1980.
  • Guided India's missile development He headed the Integrated Guided Missile Development Programme, which led to missiles such as Agni and Prithvi.
  • Principal Scientific Adviser He held the post from 1999 to 2001, advising the government on science and technology.
  • Bharat Ratna, 1997 India's highest civilian honour, alongside the Padma Bhushan (1981) and Padma Vibhushan (1990).
  • Author His books include Wings of Fire, his autobiography, and India 2020, a vision for the country's development.
